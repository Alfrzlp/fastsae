How fast is fastsae compared to sae and emdi?

Comparison of execution time, memory usage, and throughput for EBLUP and SEBLUP algorithms, across data sizes (n) from 30 to 1,000 areas.

Chart Explanations

Execution Time Chart: Shows how long each package takes to complete estimation across different sample sizes. The X-axis represents the number of areas being estimated (n), ranging from 30 to 1,000. The Y-axis shows median execution time on a logarithmic scale where each tick represents a 10x increase. A lower line indicates faster execution. For EBLUP, fastsae consistently executes in under 5 milliseconds across all sample sizes, while emdi grows from 10ms at n=30 to over 50 seconds at n=1,000.

Memory Usage: Displays peak RAM consumption during estimation. Memory is measured in megabytes on a logarithmic Y-axis. fastsae maintains minimal memory usage of approximately 0.2 MB for EBLUP and 23 MB for SEBLUP even at n=1,000. In contrast, emdi requires nearly 4 GB of memory at n=1,000, representing a 200,000x difference.

Speedup Chart: Illustrates the performance advantage of fastsae over competitors. The Y-axis displays the speedup factor on a logarithmic scale, where a value of 100 means fastsae is 100 times faster. Points above the dashed line at y=1 indicate fastsae is faster. For EBLUP with n=1,000, fastsae achieves a 12,343x speedup over emdi and a 34x speedup over sae.

Key Findings: The speedup factor ranges from 19x at small sample sizes (n=30) to 12,343x at large sample sizes (n=1,000) when compared to emdi. Memory consumption follows a similar pattern, with fastsae using 100 to 20,000 times less RAM depending on sample size and algorithm type.
